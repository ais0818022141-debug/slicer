## 2. The Cognitive Science & Evidence Against Cramming

### 1. The Fluency Illusion (Nate Kornell, 2009)

* **Finding:** Cognitive psychologist Nate Kornell found that **72% of students who crammed** genuinely believed cramming was more effective than distributing learning over time [[01:23](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D83)].
* **Reality:** When test scores were evaluated, cramming lost consistently [[01:38](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D98)].
* **Core Takeaway:** Cramming creates **familiarity**, not **retention** [[01:46](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D106)]. Recognizing information while reading is not the same as being able to recall and apply it.

### 2. The Forgetting Curve (Hermann Ebbinghaus, 1885)

* **Finding:** Without deliberate, spaced efforts to revisit newly learned information, humans forget approximately **70% of what was learned within 24 hours** [[01:54](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D114)].

## 3. The 3-Phase Study System

The framework consists of three sequential phases: **Learn**, **Retain**, and **Apply** [[02:58](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D178)].

```
┌──────────────┐     ┌──────────────┐     ┌──────────────┐
│ 1. LEARN     │ ──> │ 2. RETAIN    │ ──> │ 3. APPLY     │
│ High-yield   │     │ Spaced       │     │ Active       │
│ first        │     │ repetition   │     │ retrieval    │
│ exposure     │     │ & review     │     │ & practice Qs│
└──────────────┘     └──────────────┘     └──────────────┘

```

---

### Phase 1: LEARN (Efficient, High-Quality First Exposure)

* **Objective:** Achieve high-quality initial exposure in the shortest amount of time [[03:06](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D186)].
* **The Method:**
* Replaced passive 2-to-3 hour institutional lectures with concise, high-yield third-party video resources (e.g., *Sketchy*, *Boards and Beyond*), which took only 10 to 30 minutes for the same material [[03:13](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D193)].


* **Scientific Evidence — Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (Richard Mayer):**
* Decades of research show the human brain processes visual and auditory information through **separate processing channels** [[03:25](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D205)].
* When a learning medium simultaneously coordinates both channels (e.g., spoken narrative combined with synchronized visuals and animations), the brain absorbs substantially more without overloading either channel [[03:39](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D219)].


* **Rule:** First exposure alone is *never* sufficient for mastery, regardless of how well-designed the resource is [[04:08](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D248)].

### Phase 2: RETAIN (Systematic Spaced Repetition)

* **Objective:** Convert initial exposure into permanent long-term memory and overcome the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve [[04:14](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D254)].
* **Tools & Setup:**
* **Anki** with the pre-made **AnKing** deck [[04:33](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D273)].
* The deck is tagged and indexed to resources like *Sketchy* and *Boards and Beyond*, creating a nearly frictionless transition from watching a video to testing corresponding flashcards [[04:39](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D279)].



#### The Daily Workflow

1. **Morning ("Eat That Frog"):**
* Wake up early to complete all due Anki review cards *before* class or hospital rotations [[04:55](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D295)].
* Grounded in Brian Tracy’s principle in *Eat That Frog*: tackle the most critical, difficult task first thing in the morning [[05:11](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D311)].


2. **Daytime:**
* Attend mandatory classes, clinical duties, or hospital rotations [[05:27](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D327)].


3. **Evening:**
* Watch high-yield videos covering the *next* day’s topics [[05:27](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D327)].
* Unsuspend and complete the specific Anki cards matching those videos [[05:31](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D331)].



#### The 3x Exposure Advantage

Under this schedule, new material is encountered **3 separate times before ever hearing it in class** [[05:36](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D336)]:

1. First pass watching the evening video [[05:40](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D340)].
2. Second pass doing the new Anki cards that same evening [[05:40](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D340)].
3. Third pass reviewing the cards the following morning [[05:40](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D340)].

#### Scientific Evidence & Long-Term Continuity

* **Evidence — *Make It Stick* (Peter Brown, Henry Roediger, Mark McDaniel, 2014):** Spacing out study sessions forces the brain to reconsolidate memories, strengthening neural pathways linking new facts to existing knowledge [[05:47](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D347)].
* **Cumulative Review:** Never suspend old decks when a course ends. Even after finishing a block (e.g., Biochemistry), review cards continue daily through Cardiology, Immunology, and subsequent rotations to ensure indefinite retention [[06:15](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D375)].

### Phase 3: APPLY (Active Retrieval & Exam Simulation)

* **Objective:** Bridge the gap between memorizing discrete facts and executing diagnostic clinical decision-making on test day [[09:23](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D563)].
* **The Analogy:** You cannot learn to play basketball solely by studying rulebooks or watching drills; you must get on the court and shoot. Test-taking is an independent skill requiring targeted practice questions [[09:23](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D563)].
* **Tool Used:** **UWorld** (for MCAT and USMLE preparation) [[09:33](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D573)].

#### Scientific Evidence Supporting Testing

1. **Utility Ranking of 10 Learning Techniques (John Dunlosky et al.):**
* Evaluated 10 commonly used study strategies [[09:43](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D583)].
* **Practice testing** earned one of only two **high utility** ratings [[09:50](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D590)].
* **Rereading** (the most prevalent student technique) earned a **low utility** rating due to passive familiarity [[09:50](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D590)].


2. **The Testing Effect (Henry Roediger & Jeffrey Karpicke):**
* Proved that the mental effort of pulling information out of memory strengthens neural storage far more than re-encoding or reading it [[10:12](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D612)].
* **Failure as Diagnosis:** Missing a practice question exposes hidden conceptual blind spots and knowledge gaps before the real exam exposes them [[10:25](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D625)].



#### The 1–2 Week Buffer Strategy

* Progress slightly faster than the class schedule each day by watching upcoming lectures and unlocking cards ahead of time [[10:36](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D636)].
* By the final **1 to 2 weeks before the exam**, 100% of new material has been learned and reviewed [[10:47](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D647)].
* Dedicated study time shifts completely away from new cards and videos into **rigorous practice questions** [[10:52](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D652)].
* Exam day becomes a process of refinement rather than panic or cramming [[10:58](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D658)].

## 4. Key Takeaways & Core Principles

| Phase | Core Mechanism | Cognitive Science / Evidence | Practical Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Learn** | Fast, high-yield dual-channel exposure | **Richard Mayer** (Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning) | High-yield video (e.g., Sketchy, Boards & Beyond) over 2–3 hr lectures |
| **2. Retain** | Systematic spaced repetition | **Hermann Ebbinghaus** (Forgetting Curve); **Brown, Roediger, & McDaniel** (*Make It Stick*) | Anki (AnKing deck); morning reviews + evening new cards |
| **3. Apply** | Active retrieval & gap finding | **John Dunlosky et al.** (Study utility review); **Roediger & Karpicke** (Testing Effect) | Question banks (e.g., UWorld); dedicated practice in the final 1–2 weeks |

* **Process Over Product:** Specific tools (*Sketchy*, *Anki*, *UWorld*) were tailored to medicine, but the underlying process applies universally to any demanding field (law, engineering, skill acquisition) [[11:09](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D669)].
* **Universal Psychology:** The forgetting curve treats everyone equally; mastering any field requires moving past passive familiarity into disciplined, evidence-based retention and application [[11:36](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3Ds-AwBQsQwxM%26t%3D696)].
